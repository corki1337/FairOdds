THE PROBLEM

The house wins.
Then it limits the winners.

  • Every bookmaker market carries a built-in margin. The odds are priced so the house profits on average.
  • Players who win consistently get stake-limited or closed. The bookmaker is the counterparty, so a winner is a cost.
  • Betting with friends runs on trust. Group chats, screenshots and IOUs that someone forgets to pay.
FairOdds02 / 13
THE INSIGHT

When fans bet against each other, there is no house to protect.

  • In a shared pool, winners are paid by the other side of the pool. Nobody loses money because you are good.
  • Sports betting is already social. The bet happens in the group chat; only the payment is missing.
  • On Solana a $1 bet costs a fraction of a cent in fees, and a link can carry a transaction.

TRUST BY DESIGN

Nobody holds the money.
Nobody picks the result.

  • Program-owned vaults. Stakes sit in a PDA, not in our wallet. Payout = stake × pool ÷ winning side.
  • Pinned oracle. A Switchboard feed reads the final score from TheSportsDB; its job hash is fixed when the market is created.
  • Permissionless settlement. Anyone can lock, resolve or claim. No admin button decides the winner.
  • Automatic refunds. Draw, one-sided pool at kickoff, or no result before the deadline: everyone gets their stake back.

WHY NOW, WHY SOLANA

Prediction markets went mainstream. The long tail of matches did not.

  • Order books need market makers, so lower leagues and friendly rivalries stay empty. A pool works with two people.
  • Solana Actions turn any URL into a bet slip, the natural format for group chats and social feeds.
  • Sub-cent fees and stablecoins make small, frequent bets between friends viable.

BUSINESS MODEL

Paid only when someone wins.

  • 0.5% of the winners' profit on match pools. Stakes and refunds are never charged. On a balanced pool that is about 0.25% of volume.
  • Example: $10M monthly volume ≈ $25k monthly revenue. The fee can rise to 1–2% and stay far below a bookmaker margin.
  • Season pools: 0% fee; revenue comes from yield on principal locked for months (demo reserve today, lending protocol later).
  • Next: host fees for creator-run pools and a paid API for bots and media partners.

GO-TO-MARKET

Every bet needs an opponent. That is the growth loop.

RISKS WE PLAN FOR

We know where the hard part is.

  • Regulation. Real-money pools only where licensed: geofencing and a licensed partner per market; a points-based free-to-play mode elsewhere.
  • Oracle failure. Pinned feeds, two independent sources for seasons, and refunds by deadline instead of manual verdicts.
  • Empty pools. One-sided pools cancel at kickoff; challenge links and the bot API bring the counterparty.
  • Today: devnet only, demo tokens with no monetary value.
